# Fundraising brief — market, ask, sources (2018–2026)

Internal and donor-facing. Not a public website page. Not father-facing copy.
Every numbered source is dated August 2018 through August 2026.
POSITIONING.md governs price, vocabulary, and claims. If this brief and
that file disagree, that file wins.

---

## 1. The sentence

Fathers.com is how a program gives a man a fathering course, a Leader, and a
certificate a court or funder can check — and how the man never gets a bill.

The National Center for Fathering sells the credential. The participant
completes free. That inversion is the product.

## 2. Why this is fundable now

Three checks cleared in the last eight years. None of them require us to
pretend the curriculum is already rated.

**The federal fatherhood market just reset.** On 29–30 September 2025 ACF
awarded a new HMRF cohort: 109 organizations in 38 states, five years through
29 September 2030, **$150 million a year**. Fifty of those awards are FORGE
Fatherhood. FORGE buys parenting skill, healthy-relationship education,
economic-stability services, and nFORM reporting. It also names fathers close
to release or just home. Certification, Leader training, and an Efficacy
Report sit on lines those grantees already write. [1][2][3]

**Programs still buy binders. Nobody sells a registry.** The incumbent 24:7
Dad facilitator kit publishes at **$899** (2026 list). Training is optional.
There is no exam, no annual renewal, no public revocation. Prepare/Enrich is
the couple-assessment analog, not a fathering credential. NCF’s launch prices
— Certified Facilitator $349 plus $99/year, Certified Organization $1,500 per
site per year — are a priced hypothesis for the thing a kit cannot include:
supervision, a registry, and revocation that stays visible. [4][5]

**The reimbursement door is empty.** Under Family First, a Title IV-E
Prevention Services Clearinghouse rating is what makes a program eligible for
federal child-welfare reimbursement. In July 2025 the Clearinghouse reviewed
24:7 Dad and InsideOut Dad and rated both **Does Not Currently Meet Criteria**
(no eligible studies). No fatherhood curriculum holds Promising or better.
That is the lane, not a trophy. This gift pays the measurement spine a later
comparison-group study needs. It does not buy a rating. [6][7][8]

Adjacent money, same eight years:

- Opioid settlements obligated to states and localities exceed **$50 billion**.
  Several state MOUs already list family recovery support, parenting, and
  provider training as approved uses. [9][10]
- The Second Chance Act has made **148** awards for incarcerated parents and
  their families (family-based SUD treatment, young parents, fatherhood /
  motherhood reentry). Those solicitations prefer independently evaluated
  approaches. [11]
- DoD counts **888,892** active-duty children; **40.5%** are age 0–5
  (2023). The military surface stays dark until rehab completion is real.
  The later door is a unit or chaplain, not a public veterans brand. [12]

## 3. What the last eight years say we should build

The literature does not rate Coming Home or Keystone. It does say what a
fatherhood product has to be if a funder is going to keep paying for it.

**Engagement still matters.** Reviews from 2019–2024 keep finding that
father involvement — especially quality of engagement, not hours on a
timesheet — is associated with children’s cognitive, language, social, and
emotional development. [13][14][15][16]

**Programs move parenting a little. Co-parenting most.** Holmes and
colleagues (2020) meta-analyzed responsible-fatherhood programs for
unmarried, low-income, nonresident fathers. Overall effect *d* = 0.099.
Significant for involvement, parenting, and co-parenting. Strongest on
co-parenting (*d* = 0.167). Not significant for employment or child support.
[17]

**What inside the program enlarges those effects.** ACF’s Fatherhood TIES
meta-analysis (OPRE 2024-09) found larger parenting, relationship, and
well-being effects when content is parenting knowledge/skills or the father’s
role, and when delivery is individual rather than group-only. Implementation
problems shrink economic effects. [18]

That is the product shape: a short film, a check-in, a promised action, a
later “did you use it,” and a Leader who can see who stalled. Group night
is optional. The practice is not.

**Rehab houses will take fathering if someone puts it on the unit.** Stover,
McMahon, and Moore (2019) randomized 62 fathers in six-month residential
substance-use programs to two fatherhood interventions. Both were possible
and welcomed. Both reduced anger and affect dysregulation. Men’s residential
treatment still typically omits parenting. [19] Statewide fathering-focused
implementation for child-welfare-referred men continued into 2022–2023. That
is demand from the agencies that already sit next to the house. It is not
our outcome. [20][21]

**Armed-forces dads will take skill. They will not take a clinic brand.**
Follow-on ADAPT trial papers (2020–2021) show military families use a
parenting program after deployment when it is tools, not a behavioral-health
label. Fathers with more emotion-regulation difficulty showed larger
parenting gains. Father PTSD reduced the main effect in earlier work — which
is why a Leader’s job includes a same-day handoff, and why we do not treat.
[22][23] VA’s *Parenting for Veterans* (updated January 2026) is free, with
no login, on purpose. DoD/Penn State Thrive added *Father Forward* as a
two-hour father module: role, absence, split households, work and family.
[24][25] Fort Carson’s 2026 write-up: when someone invited fathers on a
schedule, they came. Clinics still only screen the birth mother. [26]

We do not flip a military marketing surface on the back of that. Rehab
completion first. `SHOW_MILITARY` stays off.

**Community and church groups are the same product, different tone.** FORGE
grantees, reentry nonprofits, and voluntary clubs already buy curriculum.
Open participation (men set the pace) is a wording change, not a fourth
vertical. There is no separate 2018–2026 outcome literature for F3 or
breakfast groups. Cite Holmes and TIES, not a men’s-club study we do not
have.

## 4. The offer (launch prices — hypothesis)

| What | Who pays | Price | Notes |
|---|---|---|---|
| Profile, courses, Certificate of Completion | The man | $0 | Non-negotiable |
| Certified Facilitator | The organization or a donor | $349, then $99/year | Exam, supervised first cohort, code, public registry, revocable |
| Certified Organization | The site or a donor | $1,500 / site / year | Unlimited cohorts at that site. Efficacy Report included |
| Seat gift | — | Retired | Do not revive. Never fund a man as a line item |
| Library membership | Optional individual | $79 founding / $120 after | Film production only. Never called sponsorship |

What a kit cannot include, and what the annual fee is for: the registry
listing, revocation that stays visible, Leader renewal, and one page of
cohort numbers a grant officer can attach.

## 5. The $260,000 founding gift

A working year-one package. Replace the split when a named donor prefers a
single line (films only, credentials only, or the measurement spine).

| Line | Amount | Deliverable |
|---|---:|---|
| Films | $140,000 | Coming Home Present and Steady Under Pressure. Same Team waits. |
| Credentials | $33,000 | 15 organization certifications and 30 Leader credentials. Donor pays the SKU. |
| Measurement spine | $45,000 | Intake baseline, exit retake, nFORM-shaped definitions, external-evaluator design |
| Supervised cohorts | $27,000 | Coaching the first Leaders through the required first cohort |
| Reserve | $15,000 | Hold for production or a second house |
| **Total** | **$260,000** | |

What this gift is not: a Clearinghouse study, a military launch, Stripe, or
a seat scholarship. Second Chance and later foundation RFPs can fund the
comparison-group evaluation. This gift makes that study designable.

What success looks like in year one (educational, not clinical):

- Houses running Film → Check-in → Action with a named Leader
- Certificates issued and checkable
- Efficacy pages a FORGE or settlement officer can read without seeing a man
- A written protocol for the later comparison-group study

Renewal above 70% in the first paid cycle would support the annual site fee.
Below 40% converts it to one-time-plus-training. Those thresholds are
doctrine, not data we already have.

## 6. Talk track (ten minutes)

1. The man pays nothing. You are funding the credential and the films.
2. Fifty FORGE grantees just started a five-year spend that already includes
   parenting skill and staff training. We are the course and the registry,
   not a competing grant.
3. Fatherhood programs work a little, on parenting and co-parenting, when
   the content is skill and someone stays with the man. That is the last
   eight years, not our brochure.
4. The houses want this in the unit. Military units will want it later, in
   training language, after we can show completion.
5. No fatherhood curriculum is rated for Title IV-E. We will not claim a
   rating. We will build the file a rater can read.
6. $260,000. Films, fifteen sites, thirty Leaders, the spine.

If they ask “are you evidence-based?”: No. The incumbents are unrated. We
are built for that bar. The honest sentence is the one that gets the gift.

If they ask for a military logo: Not yet. 888,000 active-duty children is
the later market, not this raise.

If they want to sponsor seats: No. Pay the site and the Leader.

## 8. Leave out of the packet

- Any source before August 2018, including Canfield 1994, Sarkadi 2008, and
  the 2016 residential 77% survey. Those remain internal history. They are
  not this raise.
- “Evidence-based,” “clinically proven,” norms counts, and Keystone item
  counts.
- Relapse, PTSD reduction, or child-maltreatment reduction as an NCF result.
- A public military or veterans brand.
- A $120 seat.
- A claim that we are first or only on certificate verification.
