FILM COURSE · 12 SESSIONS · ~12 MINUTES EACH

Coming Home Present

FOR FATHERS IN REHAB OR TREATMENT RECONNECTING AT HOME

Twelve short films for fathers in rehab or treatment preparing to reconnect at home. Built so every practice fits the rules that govern you now. Same body skills if you are coming home from service.

Self-paced. About twelve minutes each. A Certified Facilitator is available when your seat is claimed.

Start with free ProfileWatch the preview player

Preview is practice only. Earn proof through a Certified Organization.

Important notes

Completion never requires contact with your child. No practice asks you to go beyond the rules that govern you now or any court order. Where contact is limited, every drill has a before-return option.

SESSION 1~12 MIN

The Body You Bring Home

SHAPE PREVIEW · PLACEHOLDER

Final film goes here. Until then this shape still holds the 16:9 slot.

Open in the player

“Your body did its job there. Now teach it that home is not there.”

One scene. A door slams down the hall. Before a thought arrives, his shoulders are up and his eyes are on the doorway. It is only the kid going back for shoes.

In the room. The body's alarm in plain language: how a long stretch in a controlled environment retunes it, and how that collides with a loud home. You plot your baseline and pick your tells.

What you leave with. You can name what your body is doing when it revs, and call it a physical response, not a character flaw.

THE PRACTICE

Before the return: Keep a three-times-daily one-word body-state log where you are now. Name two likely home triggers (kids, partner, kitchen noise). Rehearse the sentence you will use to tell family what helps.

After the return: Keep the log at home, agree one signal with a family member if contact allows, and report what happened.

The science in the room: Fight or flight can outlast the threat; naming the response is the first retraining step.

SESSION 2~12 MIN

Home Is Not There

SHAPE PREVIEW · PLACEHOLDER

Final film goes here. Until then this shape still holds the 16:9 slot.

Open in the player

“Same noise, new meaning. Train the difference.”

One scene. A shout from another room used to mean danger. At home it is a kid looking for a shoe. Same sound. He practices labeling it as home until his shoulders stay down.

In the room. You train the difference between there and home with concrete sounds and scenes. Rehearsal gives the body a new meaning before the live test.

What you leave with. You can catch a home sound and label it as home on purpose.

THE PRACTICE

Before the return: List two sounds or scenes from home (kids, partner, street) and write the new meaning next to each. Rehearse the labels aloud daily for five days inside program rules.

After the return: When one of those sounds hits, say the home label once under your breath and log whether your body stood down.

The science in the room: Retraining meaning reduces false alarms when old cues meet a safe setting.

SESSION 3~12 MIN

Plan Around the Wave

SHAPE PREVIEW · PLACEHOLDER

Final film goes here. Until then this shape still holds the 16:9 slot.

Open in the player

“Plan around the wave. Do not grade yourself by it.”

One scene. Day nine home. He slept badly, snapped over spilled cereal, and by noon he is quietly deciding he is failing. He is not failing. He is in a wave, and the plan says what to do on wave days.

In the room. Energy, mood, and sleep run in waves in the early weeks of any return from a program or from service. That is expected. You plan around it instead of reading it as a verdict on the reconnect.

What you leave with. You can expect the low weeks, plan around them, and stop grading yourself by them.

THE PRACTICE

Before the return: Hold one fixed sleep and wake time for five nights in the house. Write your wave-day rule on one page for kids and partner contact.

After the return: Hold the same sleep anchor at home and use the wave-day rule on the next hard day.

The science in the room: Early-return waves in sleep and mood are common while a body leaves an imposed routine.

SESSION 4~12 MIN

Few Promises, Kept

SHAPE PREVIEW · PLACEHOLDER

Final film goes here. Until then this shape still holds the 16:9 slot.

Open in the player

“Few commitments, kept without fail.”

One scene. He wants to promise everything. He picks two standing times instead and keeps both. By week three the child stops asking if he means it.

In the room. Distance and early return punish over-promising. You choose a short list you can keep from where you actually are (program rules, court orders, duty schedule), then keep it without fail.

What you leave with. You can make fewer promises and keep every one you make.

THE PRACTICE

Before the return: Make and keep up to three small promises deliverable inside your current rules. Where no contact is permitted, keep promises to your facilitator or cohort and file the child- and partner-facing list for day one.

After the return: Keep three small specific promises in a week at home and log each one.

The science in the room: Reliability beats volume; each kept promise is evidence.

SESSION 5~12 MIN

The Child Who Grew

SHAPE PREVIEW · PLACEHOLDER

Final film goes here. Until then this shape still holds the 16:9 slot.

Open in the player

“Meet the child in front of you, not the one you left.”

One scene. He left a child who held his hand crossing the street. On the phone now, the child talks about a teacher he has never heard of and a best friend whose name is new.

In the room. You map where your child actually is now against the child you left, then build questions that rebuild a current picture: friends, fears, interests.

What you leave with. You can recalibrate to the child who exists today.

THE PRACTICE

Before the return: Build a one-page current picture from photos, letters, calls, and what others report. Draft ten questions with no advice attached. Use as many as one permitted call or visit allows.

After the return: Hold one ten-minute questions-only conversation. Log three things you learned.

The science in the room: Children hit growth milestones on a clock; knowing the present child is the base of attunement.

SESSION 6~12 MIN

Ask Before You Assume

SHAPE PREVIEW · PLACEHOLDER

Final film goes here. Until then this shape still holds the 16:9 slot.

Open in the player

“Ask once. Listen longer than you talk.”

One scene. He almost fills the silence with advice. He asks one real question instead, then shuts up long enough to hear the answer. The child talks more in two minutes than in the last month of lectures.

In the room. Assumptions belong to the child you left. You practice one clean ask and a longer listen, then reflect one thing you heard.

What you leave with. You can ask once and listen longer than you talk.

THE PRACTICE

Before the return: On a permitted call or visit, ask one question and listen longer than you talk. Where no contact is permitted, rehearse with a cohort partner and write the question for day one.

After the return: Ask one question in person, listen longer than you talk, and log what you reflected back.

The science in the room: Listening longer than you talk rebuilds a current map of the child.

SESSION 7~12 MIN

Small Deposits

SHAPE PREVIEW · PLACEHOLDER

Final film goes here. Until then this shape still holds the 16:9 slot.

Open in the player

“Small and often beats big and rare.”

One scene. Mid-conversation with someone else, the child appears holding a drawing. He stops, looks at it, and says show me the best part. Twenty seconds. That is a deposit.

In the room. Trust rebuilds through many small responsive interactions with kids and partner, not one big make-up moment. You practice catching and returning bids for attention.

What you leave with. You can make daily small deposits and explain why they beat grand gestures.

THE PRACTICE

Before the return: Use one 60-second settling routine before each call or visit. Catch and return three bids during one permitted contact. Where no contact is permitted, role-play five exchanges with a cohort partner.

After the return: Settle first, then run five exchanges a day for a week and tally them.

The science in the room: Serve and return: a child's bid met by a steady adult builds trust and brains.

SESSION 8~12 MIN

Frequency Beats Intensity

SHAPE PREVIEW · PLACEHOLDER

Final film goes here. Until then this shape still holds the 16:9 slot.

Open in the player

“Show up short and steady.”

One scene. He used to wait for a perfect long visit. He switches to short, on-time contact. The child starts setting a place for the call without being asked.

In the room. Predictable short contact beats rare dramatic presence. You design a frequency you can keep from where you stand.

What you leave with. You can show up short and steady on a schedule the child can trust.

THE PRACTICE

Before the return: Set one repeatable short contact ritual inside current rules. Where no contact is permitted, build the ritual on paper and file it for day one.

After the return: Run the short ritual twice from home in one week and keep the times exact.

The science in the room: Predictability lowers a child's background stress more than occasional intensity.

SESSION 9~12 MIN

When It Breaks

SHAPE PREVIEW · PLACEHOLDER

Final film goes here. Until then this shape still holds the 16:9 slot.

Open in the player

“Rupture is normal. Repair is the skill.”

One scene. He promised a call at seven. The line was long and the call went out at nine. The next call opens with the repair, said plainly, no excuses attached.

In the room. Every close relationship ruptures. Repair, not perfection, builds security. You practice a plain repair script until it comes out steady.

What you leave with. You can come back after a bad moment and repair it out loud.

THE PRACTICE

Before the return: Write the repair for one past rupture in your own words, whether or not it can be sent. Rehearse it aloud with a cohort partner or facilitator.

After the return: Perform one deliberate repair after a rupture and record what you said.

The science in the room: Reliable repair, not perfection, is what builds security.

SESSION 10~12 MIN

Repair Without Pride

SHAPE PREVIEW · PLACEHOLDER

Final film goes here. Until then this shape still holds the 16:9 slot.

Open in the player

“Go first. Keep it short.”

One scene. Pride wants him to wait. He goes first instead: I was wrong to raise my voice. You did not deserve that. Short. No defense. The air changes.

In the room. You practice going first with a short repair. Pride delays the skill; going first protects the bond.

What you leave with. You can go first with a short repair when pride wants to wait.

THE PRACTICE

Before the return: Write a go-first repair in under twenty words. Rehearse until it is steady. Deliver through a permitted channel if allowed, otherwise file it.

After the return: Go first on the next miss. Keep it short. Log the words.

The science in the room: Going first models responsibility; delay teaches the child to carry the silence.

SESSION 11~12 MIN

Reunion Day

SHAPE PREVIEW · PLACEHOLDER

Final film goes here. Until then this shape still holds the 16:9 slot.

Open in the player

“If the child pulls away, that is the start, not the answer.”

One scene. First hour home. The younger one hides behind a leg. The older one shrugs off the hug. He keeps his voice low and offers the small plan he rehearsed: a walk to the corner and back.

In the room. You prepare for the first hours back with kids and/or a significant other, including early pull-away. You write and rehearse a low-pressure first-hour plan. Same climax work if you are coming home from service.

What you leave with. You leave with a realistic, rehearsed first-hour plan and the patience to let it be imperfect.

THE PRACTICE

Before the return: Write the low-pressure first-hour plan (kids and/or partner) and rehearse it aloud. Your facilitator checks the plan.

After the return: Run the plan and report how it went.

The science in the room: Early pull-away at reunions is common and often temporary; patience is a skill.

SESSION 12~12 MIN

The Season of Return

SHAPE PREVIEW · PLACEHOLDER

Final film goes here. Until then this shape still holds the 16:9 slot.

Open in the player

“The return is a season, not a day.”

One scene. Week six, an ordinary Tuesday. Nothing dramatic happened. He did the ritual anyway, logged the wave, made the small deposit. That is the plan working.

In the room. You fold the course into a personal return plan for fathering and partner reconnect, frame the work as a long season, and name an accountability partner. Same season frame after service.

What you leave with. You leave with a written season plan, a first checkpoint, and a named accountability partner.

THE PRACTICE

Before the return: Complete the one-page return plan, name the accountability partner, and set the first after-return checkpoint date.

After the return: Work the plan and report at the checkpoint.

The science in the room: Routines hold gains; steady spaced practice beats bursts.
